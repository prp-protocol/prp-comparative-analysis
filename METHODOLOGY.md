# Evidence methodology

This document describes the accepted annotation policy; it introduces no new criterion, verdict or requirement.

Capability verdicts and evidence levels answer different questions. Yes and No apply to the selected stack and criterion scope. Pending means the evidence is not established. An evidence annotation does not automatically change a verdict.

The four existing levels are:

- Assumed: plausible inference from architecture; premises and limits must be explicit.
- Reported: explicit attributable statement without enough inspected documentation to independently establish the mechanism or finding.
- Documented: identifiable specification or source code supports the decision within the selected scope.
- Demonstrated: reproducible execution evidence supports the decision within the stated test scope.

Unclassified is a temporary annotation state, not a fifth level and not equivalent to Pending. Evidence basis is not protocol maturity or a quality ranking. A demonstration may support Yes or No; partial coverage must be disclosed. Authorship and independence are separate from the level.

An annotation fingerprint binds the decision, criterion and selected profile. A changed fingerprint invalidates that annotation for reuse until review, without changing capability verdicts. Retain scope, basis, attribution, coverage and review date.

The accepted display rule hides the evidence band for demonstrated Yes or reported/documented/demonstrated No. Other bands remain distinct, including Pending. Accessible labels and cell details retain the level and qualifications. This is a description of the existing policy, not an instruction to deploy a website change.

A component test does not establish full selected-profile operation, interoperability, global scale or independent adoption. Historical receipts remain evidence of their recorded scope; they are not claims of a new execution.
